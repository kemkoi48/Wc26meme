Reading the tape · MEDS, 16 Sep 2026

What the candles said, hour by hour

Thirty-nine real ten-minute bars, regular session only. Each candle shows four prices: where the ten minutes opened, where it closed, and the highest and lowest anything traded in between. A filled body means it closed below its open. The thin lines above and below — wicks — are the prices that were reached and then rejected. Hover any candle for its exact numbers.

Bullish engulfing

10:40am ET · textbook

The 10:30 bar closed red at $5.4999. The next bar opened below that close ($5.4950) and closed at $6.67 — above the whole previous body. That is the pattern: sellers had control for ten minutes, and buyers took all of it back plus more. Price ran to $12.31 within twenty-five minutes.

The $12.31 spike

10:55am ET · long upper wick

High $12.31, close $10.03 on 3.3M shares. $2.28 of that move was rejected inside five minutes. A long upper wick on the biggest bar of a run is where the sellers first show up — the price was reached, and it did not hold.

Shooting star

11:10am ET · the day's last high

Small body ($9.24 → $9.83) parked at the bottom of a tall range that reached $11.32 — an upper wick 2.5× the body. Textbook exhaustion. This is the exact bar in which the scanner logged MEDS as making “a fourth new high.” The candle was saying rejection while the log was saying strength. The candle was right.

The dead bar

11:30am ET · 5,621 shares

A near-flat candle on 0.1% of the previous bar's volume. Volume that vanishes mid-session in a halting microcap usually means the stock was halted, not that it went quiet. Do not read a pattern into a bar where nothing traded.

Shapes and what they claim to mean

The candle patterns worth knowing

Seven shapes that recur constantly. Each one is a story about who won the last few minutes — buyers or sellers — told through where the price opened, closed, and how far it got in between. Read the caveats: none of these is a prediction, and every one of them fails routinely.

The rule that matters more than any shape: a pattern on low volume is noise. The same candle means something completely different on 3 million shares than on 5,000. Every pattern below should be read together with the volume bar underneath it, and with where it sits — a hammer at the bottom of a long decline is a signal; a hammer in the middle of a range is a candle.

Plain English

The indicators, and the exact numbers this account uses

Every indicator below is used somewhere in this system. The third column is not a textbook default — it is the real threshold this account trades on, and where that number came from.

RVOL

Relative volume

Today's volume compared with a normal day's volume at the same time. RVOL of 5 means five times the usual crowd is in the room. It is the single best answer to “is anything actually happening here, or is this one trade?”

HereElevated = real money, not one print. Before 9:30am the broker's own field is a broken flat “1”, so it is recomputed from raw bars every time.

Float

Shares available to trade

Not all shares outstanding — only the ones actually free to change hands. A small float means a small amount of buying moves the price a long way, in both directions. It is why these names go up 300% and give it all back.

HereUnder 20M to qualify. WAFU's was 114,503 — its premarket volume was 9.5× the entire float.

RSI

Relative strength index, 0–100

How much of recent movement has been upward. Above 70 is conventionally “overbought”, below 30 “oversold”. The common mistake is reading it as a sell signal — a strong trend can sit above 70 for weeks.

HereEntry band 50–70 on the growth screen. Deliberately not above 70: that is buying something already extended.

ADX

Average directional index

Measures how strong a trend is, not which way it points. A high ADX on a falling stock means a strong downtrend. This is the indicator most often misread as bullish.

HereAbove 25 = a real trend. Below 25 no trend signal is acted on at all. CRUS was rejected with ADX 38 because the trend it measured was down.

MACD

Moving average convergence divergence

Two moving averages and the gap between them. The histogram is that gap drawn as bars: shrinking bars mean momentum is fading, a flip through zero means it has changed direction.

HereHistogram direction is logged on every open position daily. A negative histogram shrinking five sessions running is momentum converging back, not against.

VWAP

Volume-weighted average price

The average price everyone actually paid today, weighted by size. Above it, the average buyer is up; below it, they are down. That is why it acts as a line traders defend.

HereNot currently a formal gate — the “still near the day's high” structure test does similar work. Listed because you will see it referenced everywhere.

ATR

Average true range

How far this stock typically moves in a day, in dollars. It is how you size a stop that is not hit by ordinary noise. A 2% stop on a name with a 12% daily range will be taken out by nothing at all.

HereThe growth sleeve's 18% trailing stop is deliberately wide for exactly this reason — it is the user's stated tolerance, not a backtested number.

Dilution

ATM, shelf, S-1, 424B

Not an indicator — a filing. It means the company can legally print new shares into your rally. On a microcap this outweighs every chart pattern on this page combined.

HereMandatory check on any sub-$5 stock with a sub-1M float. Of six low-float movers checked on one day, five had live dilution mechanics. That is the norm.

How a name gets through

Three gates, and all three are hard

1

Real volume, verified against raw bars

Meaningful positive change on a float under 20M, with relative volume genuinely elevated. Before 9:30am the broker's own RVOL field is a flat “1” placeholder, so premarket figures are recomputed from historical bars — today's cumulative volume against the same time-of-day window across five prior sessions. A number that comes out of the scanner untouched is not trusted.

2

Still near the high, and time-aware about it

A name faded well off its high has already printed. But a stock within fifteen minutes of a fresh high is igniting, not finished, even if it has pulled back — a move whose high was an hour ago and never retested is the real already-printed case.

3

A catalyst, or silence

Three sources in order: Stocktwits for the lead, the Benzinga wire and Stocklake for the sourced, dated story. Chatter is never a catalyst — a squeeze rumour or an unverified price target is a thing to verify, never a thing to cite. Any sub-$5 microcap with a sub-1M float also gets its SEC filing index pulled. If nothing is found, nothing is sent, however large the move.

What this does not do

  • One session is one session. Four alerts is not a sample, and nothing here should be read as a hit rate. The gate was installed the night before this record begins.
  • It governs entry only. Every one of the four names gave back most of a much larger intraday move. There is no exit rule here at all — and this account's own order history, across 108 real trades, says positions held past roughly five minutes are net negative.
  • It will cost you winners. QCLS closed up 120% and was suppressed. That is the designed behaviour of a filter that requires a reason, not a malfunction.
  • Expect long silences. Most cycles produce no message. On this day, five of the nine hourly cycles sent nothing. Silence is the correct output, not a fault.
  • The patterns on this page are vocabulary, not edge. Every candle formation here fails routinely. They describe what just happened; they do not tell you what happens next.
  • This edition does not update itself. It is a fixed record of one closed session.
