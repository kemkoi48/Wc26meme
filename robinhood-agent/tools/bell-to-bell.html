The four things to check before you touch a ticker
  1. Relative volume, not the % gain. A big move on normal volume is thin — maybe a couple of prints. Real relative volume means money actually showed up. Sort for it in the screener; don't trust a green number on its own.
  2. Can you name the catalyst in one sentence? Not "it's green" — why. If you can't say it out loud, you're guessing what the crowd is doing and you don't know whether they're buying or selling into you.
  3. Early or already extended? Pull the ticker up in the Chart section below before entering. If price already ran and is fading off its high, the move is done — you'd be buying the exhaustion.
  4. Float cuts both ways. Tight float moves fast on real interest and reverses just as fast. Not good or bad — it means smaller size and more violence in both directions.
