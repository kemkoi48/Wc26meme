Candle patterns

Eight shapes · what they claim · how they fail

Every candle is four prices: where the period opened, where it closed, and the highest and lowest anything traded in between. A filled body closed below its open; the thin wicks are prices that were reached and rejected. These eight shapes recur constantly — each is a story about who won the last few minutes. None of them is a prediction, and every one fails routinely.

The rule that outranks every shape: a pattern on low volume is noise. The same candle means something entirely different on 3 million shares than on 5,000 — read it together with the volume bar and with where it sits. A hammer at the bottom of a long decline is a signal; a hammer mid-range is a candle.

The indicators

Plain English · with this account's real thresholds

Every indicator below is used somewhere in this system. The third column is not a textbook default — it is the real threshold this account trades on, and where the number came from.

RVOL

Relative volume

Today's volume against a normal day's at the same time. RVOL of 5 means five times the usual crowd is in the room. The best single answer to “is anything actually happening here, or is this one print?”

Here5× is the pillar. Before 9:30am the broker's own field is a broken flat “1”, so it gets recomputed from raw bars — a * marks a self-computed value.

Float

Shares free to trade

Not shares outstanding — only those actually available. A small float means a small amount of buying moves the price a long way, in both directions. It is why these names run 300% and give it all back.

HereUnder 20M to qualify. Seen as low as 114,503 — with premarket volume at 9.5× the entire float.

RSI

Relative strength index, 0–100

How much of recent movement has been upward. Above 70 is “overbought”, below 30 “oversold”. The common mistake is reading it as a sell signal — a strong trend sits above 70 for weeks.

HereEntry band 50–70 on the growth screen. Deliberately not above 70 — that is buying something already extended.

ADX

Average directional index

Measures how strong a trend is, not which way it points. A high ADX on a falling stock means a strong downtrend. The indicator most often misread as bullish.

HereAbove 25 = a real trend; below it no signal is acted on. One candidate was rejected at ADX 38 because the trend it measured was down.

MACD

Moving average convergence divergence

Two moving averages and the gap between them. The histogram draws that gap as bars: shrinking bars mean momentum fading, a flip through zero means direction changed.

HereLogged daily on every open position. A negative histogram shrinking five sessions running is momentum converging back, not against.

VWAP

Volume-weighted average price

The average price everyone actually paid today, weighted by size. Above it the average buyer is up; below it they are down. That is why it acts as a line traders defend.

HereHolding above VWAP through pullbacks is the real “still going up” tell. A single cross means little on its own.

ATR

Average true range

How far this stock typically moves in a day, in dollars. It is how you size a stop that noise will not hit. A 2% stop on a name with a 12% daily range gets taken out by nothing.

HereThe growth sleeve's 18% trailing stop is deliberately wide for this reason — a stated risk tolerance, not a backtested number.

Dilution

ATM · shelf · S-1 · 424B

Not an indicator — a filing. It means the company can legally print new shares into your rally. On a microcap this outweighs every chart pattern on this page combined.

HereMandatory on any sub-$5 stock with a sub-1M float. Of six low-float movers checked in one day, five had live dilution mechanics. That is the norm.

How the gate works

Three tests · all three hard

1 · Real volume, verified against raw bars. Meaningful positive change on a float under 20M with relative volume genuinely elevated. Before 9:30am the broker's RVOL field is a flat “1” placeholder, so premarket figures are recomputed from historical bars — today's cumulative volume against the same window across five prior sessions. A number that leaves the scanner untouched is not trusted.

2 · Still near the high, and time-aware about it. A name faded well off its high has already printed. But a stock within fifteen minutes of a fresh high is igniting, not finished, even if it has pulled back — a high set an hour ago and never retested is the real already-printed case.

3 · A catalyst, or silence. Three sources in order: Stocktwits for the lead, the Benzinga wire and Stocklake for the sourced, dated story. Chatter is never a catalyst — a squeeze rumour is a thing to verify, never a thing to cite. Any sub-$5 microcap with a sub-1M float also gets its SEC filing index pulled. If nothing is found, nothing is sent, however large the move.

Gate 3 is not automated and never will be. The board scores the two numeric gates. The catalyst is the one you check yourself, every time — that is the whole point of it.

What this board does not do

  • It places no orders. It reads your saved Robinhood scans and nothing else. A row appearing here is not a recommendation — it means the stock cleared that scan's numeric filters, no more.
  • It governs entry only. Nothing here tells you when to sell. Your own order history, across 108 round trips, shows a 60% win rate that is still net negative, because losses ran 1.6× bigger than wins — and every holding bucket past five minutes was negative. Decide the exit before you enter.
  • The two automated gates are necessary, not sufficient. A stock can clear both and still be a catalyst-free squeeze with a live dilution filing. That is exactly what gate 3 is for.
  • The patterns here are vocabulary, not edge. Every formation on this page fails routinely. They describe what just happened; they do not tell you what happens next.
  • Outside regular hours the data degrades. Relative volume goes placeholder, spreads widen and quotes go stale. The board says which session it is in at the top — believe it.
